Physically plausible retargeted motions: FlashDexRetarget on the Sharpa Wave hand

Each refined reconstruction (left) is retargeted with FlashDexRetarget: a kinematic retarget of the human hand to the Sharpa Wave hand (22 DoF on a floating wrist) gives the reference, and one reinforcement-learning policy, trained on all 10 clips at once, drives the hand in physics simulation so the simulated object follows the reconstructed one (right). Progress (from AdaDex) is the share of the clip tracked before the object first deviates by more than 5 cm or 20° from the reconstruction; a clip succeeds when the whole of it is tracked. Do-as-I-Do (tab 4) retargets the same reconstructions by sampling-based physics optimization instead (its numbers are on the round-5 reconstructions). The two viewers play independently: drag each time slider.

This run has finished: these are the rollouts of its last evaluation, at 300M steps. Late evaluations differ from one another by more than they gain (mean progress 56-72% over the last 100M steps). This one has the lowest progress of them, but smaller errors than the 250M-step evaluation shown here before: the object follows the reconstruction to 0.75 cm / 7.4° on average and ends within 3 cm of it in every clip (at 250M steps: 0.88 cm / 10.7°, and one object ended 17 cm away). AdaDex progress counts until the object first leaves 5 cm or 20° of the reconstruction. The reconstructed object orientation is off by 12-22° on average, and several reconstructed rest poses are not stable on the table in simulation (they settle 15-23°), so progress stays well below the position-only number. The hand's motion is jittery: this run's reward did not penalise fast changes of the action. A run on round 10 of the refinement, with a small reward on the action's rate of change, is training.
